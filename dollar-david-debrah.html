LESSON 01

What is a Cell?

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living things are made of one or more cells.

Learn more

Some organisms, such as many bacteria, consist of one cell. Humans, plants, and most animals are multicellular. Cells carry out life processes such as obtaining nutrients and releasing energy.

🌱
LESSON 02

Plant Cells

Plant cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus. They also usually have a cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic cells, and a large permanent vacuole.

Learn more

Cell wall: support and strength. Chloroplasts: contain chlorophyll to absorb light for photosynthesis. Vacuole: contains cell sap and helps keep the cell firm.

🐾
LESSON 03

Animal Cells

Animal cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, a nucleus, and mitochondria, among other structures.

Learn more

Animal cells do not have a cell wall or chloroplasts. Their cell membrane controls movement of substances in and out, and mitochondria release energy during aerobic respiration.

🧠
LESSON 04

Cell Parts & Functions

Different cell structures perform different jobs, working together to keep the cell alive.

Learn more

Nucleus: contains genetic material and helps control cell activities. Cytoplasm: where many chemical reactions occur. Cell membrane: controls what enters and leaves. Mitochondria: aerobic respiration.

⚔️
LESSON 05

Plant vs Animal

Both cell types share important structures, but some features differ.

Learn more

Both: cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, and mitochondria. Plant cells: cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic cells, and usually a large permanent vacuole. Animal cells lack a cell wall and chloroplasts.

🧪
LESSON 06

Cells Build Life

Cells can form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work together in organ systems.

Learn more

Example in humans: muscle cells form muscle tissue; muscle tissue forms parts of the heart; the heart works with blood vessels as part of the circulatory system.
